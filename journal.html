The Truth

Your P&L isn't the problem. Your process is.

Most traders don't lose because their strategy is bad. They lose because they can't see their own patterns — the revenge trades, the oversized positions, the setups they took out of boredom instead of edge.

A trading journal is how professionals find those leaks. Not a spreadsheet you abandon in week two. A physical journal on your desk that makes you write it down, score it, and face it — every single session.

You can't fix what you don't track.

What's Inside

Built like a professional trading operation

Six structured sections. Zero filler pages. Everything a disciplined trader logs, in the order they log it.

01

Trader Profile

Define who you are as a trader — goals, style, markets and rules — before the market tests you on all four.

02

Risk Parameters

Max risk per trade, daily loss limits, position sizing rules. Written down, so there's no negotiating with yourself mid-trade.

03

Trade Playbook

Document your 3 core setups — entry criteria, invalidation, targets. If it's not in the playbook, you don't take it.

04

Daily Log — 90 Days

Pre-market checklist, trade log, P&L and execution scoring for every session. The engine room of the journal.

05

Weekly Review

Every week: what worked, what leaked money, what gets fixed. Small corrections, compounded 12 times per quarter.

06

Monthly Statistics

Win rate, average R, best and worst setups. Your own trading data — the only backtest that actually matters.

The Daily Routine

Four steps. Every session. No exceptions.

Before the open

Prepare

Run the pre-market checklist. Levels marked, setups identified, risk defined.

During the session

Log

Record every trade as it happens — entry, size, stop, reason.

Per trade

Score

Grade your execution, not your P&L. Good process, bad outcome still scores well.

After the close

Review

Post-market review. What did the market teach you today? Write it down.

Questions

Frequently asked

Who is this journal for?

Day traders and swing traders in any market — stocks, indices, forex, crypto. If you take trades and want to stop repeating the same mistakes, it's built for you. Complete beginners can use it from day one; the structure teaches the process.

Why a physical journal instead of an app or spreadsheet?

Writing by hand forces you to slow down and actually process the trade. Apps get ignored; a journal sitting on your desk next to your keyboard doesn't. The friction is the feature.

How long does one journal last?

90 trading days — roughly one quarter of active trading, or longer if you don't trade daily. 100 pages total including profile, playbook, weekly reviews and monthly statistics.
